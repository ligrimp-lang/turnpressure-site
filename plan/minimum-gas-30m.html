Minimum gas from 30 m

Turn Pressure — dive planner & gas calculator

Two divers sharing one supply from 30 m need a fixed volume of gas to reach the surface with a safety stop. A small cylinder turns that volume into a much larger fraction of its working pressure than a large one does — the pressure answer is not one number, it depends on what is on your back.

The figures

  • 15-litre steel cylinder: 64 bar.
  • Aluminium 80 (11.1-litre): 86 bar.

Same depth, same two divers, same ascent — 22 bar apart, because the smaller cylinder holds less gas per bar.

Inputs used

  • Depth: 30 m
  • Ascent style: NDL with safety stop (9 m/min to 5 m, 3 minutes at 5 m, then to the surface)
  • Cylinders: single 15-litre steel (232 bar) and single aluminium 80 / 11.1-litre (207 bar)
  • Two divers sharing one supply, at the reserve's own ascent rate

Minimum gas does not depend on your gas strategy (thirds, halves or all-usable) or on your SAC rate — it is sized for an out-of-gas emergency at a fixed team consumption, not for how you plan to use the rest of the cylinder. Open Turn Pressure, set the depth to 30 m and pick either cylinder, and the app prints the same two figures.

Source: the app's own pinned test suite for the figures it is sold on (PublishedReserveFiguresTests.thirtyMetres), matching the corrected 30 m pair in the App Store description.

Your training, your gauge and your computer decide the dive. This is pre-dive arithmetic, not a dive computer, and it does not make any dive safe.
